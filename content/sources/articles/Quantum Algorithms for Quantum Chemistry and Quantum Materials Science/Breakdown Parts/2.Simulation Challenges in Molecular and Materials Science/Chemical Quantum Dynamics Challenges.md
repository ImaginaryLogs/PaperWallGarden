In basic chemistry, we often cheat: we pretend electrons are quantum, but we treat the nuclei (the protons and neutrons at the center) using classical Newtonian physics. However, for light atoms like Hydrogen, this cheat fails. Because protons are so light, they behave like fuzzy quantum waves. They can "tunnel" through energy barriers and exist in superpositions.

The paper highlights **Proton-Coupled Electron Transfer (PCET)**—a vital mechanism in biological enzymes (like how your body processes energy) and solar cells. In PCET, an electron and a proton jump _simultaneously_. Because the proton behaves like a quantum wave, you cannot use classical mechanics to simulate the reaction. Classical computers fail here because calculating a moving, wave-like nucleus interacting with moving, wave-like electrons creates an overwhelming explosion of probabilities.
