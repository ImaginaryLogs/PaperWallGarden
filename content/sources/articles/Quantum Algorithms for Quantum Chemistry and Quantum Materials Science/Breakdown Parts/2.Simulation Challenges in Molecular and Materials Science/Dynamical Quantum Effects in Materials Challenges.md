# Dynamical Quantum Effects in Materials Challenges
> Note: everything down here needs to be reworked.

It is already incredibly hard to find the static, resting "ground state" of a correlated material. Here, its more difficult due to the fact you want to make it move. If you shoot a laser pulse at a complex material, you knock it out of equilibrium. You trigger a cascade of "dynamical effects": electrons start hopping, the crystal lattice begins to vibrate (phonons), and those vibrations couple back into the electrons.

This is the physics behind "pump-probe" spectroscopy, ultrafast electronics, and light-induced superconductivity. To simulate this classically, you have to solve the time-dependent Schrödinger equation for a massive grid of deeply entangled particles. As time marches forward in the simulation, the quantum entanglement spreads exponentially through the material, causing classical simulation algorithms to quickly crash or run out of memory.
